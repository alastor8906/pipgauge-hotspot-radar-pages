1/ The RBA's September 29 statement raised the cash rate target by 25bp to 4.60%. That is a published fact, not an AUD/USD forecast.

2/ After a rate headline, the useful question can be numerical: if a planned loss budget is fixed, should position size shrink when the planned stop is wider?

3/ Teaching inputs: USD 10,000 account; 1% planned price risk = USD 100. AUD/USD in a USD account is approximately USD 10/pip per standard lot.

4/ Same USD 100 budget: 30-pip planned stop = about 0.33 lot. 60 pips = about 0.17 lot. 90 pips = about 0.11 lot before rounding.

5/ Keep 0.33 lot and change a planned stop from 30 to 90 pips, and planned price risk moves from roughly USD 100 to roughly USD 300 before costs. Arithmetic, not an RBA view.

6/ Spread, commission and slippage need a separately labelled hypothetical stress check. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill; a calculator verifies inputs, not policy or price direction.

7/ Sources: RBA's September 29 decision statement and ABC News Australia same-day reporting. Educational example only; not financial advice.
