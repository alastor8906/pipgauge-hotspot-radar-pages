# Tesla Fell 14.5% and Alphabet 7.1%. What Did Two Positions Lose Together?

Tesla and Alphabet reported results on the same evening. By the next close, Associated Press market coverage recorded Tesla down 14.5% and Alphabet down 7.1%.

Those are now facts, not pre-earnings scenarios. They make it possible to replace a generic “what if both fall?” table with a direct account calculation.

> Two ticker symbols became one portfolio event. The relevant result is the combined dollar loss.

## Start with equal $2,500 positions

Assume a $10,000 account held $2,500 of Tesla and $2,500 of Alphabet before the reports. These are round illustration amounts, not suggested position sizes.

The Tesla calculation is:

`$2,500 × 14.5% = $362.50 loss`

The Alphabet calculation is:

`$2,500 × 7.1% = $177.50 loss`

Combined:

`$362.50 + $177.50 = $540`

That is 5.4% of the $10,000 account in one shared event window.

| Position | Notional | AP closing move | Dollar result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tesla | $2,500 | -14.5% | -$362.50 |
| Alphabet | $2,500 | -7.1% | -$177.50 |
| Combined | $5,000 | mixed | -$540.00 |

The combined percentage should not be calculated by simply adding 14.5% and 7.1%. Each percentage applies to its own position size. Dollar losses are calculated first and then added.

## Smaller positions change the account result

If the two positions had been $1,000 each, the same closing moves would produce:

- Tesla: `$1,000 × 14.5% = $145`.
- Alphabet: `$1,000 × 7.1% = $71`.
- Combined: `$216`, or 2.16% of a $10,000 account.

The news and percentage moves are unchanged. Only the account exposure changes.

## Why the stop estimate could fail

A normal stop order expresses an intended exit. It does not guarantee a fill when earnings news moves the next tradable price beyond the stop.

That is why the useful comparison is between:

1. the planned loss before the report;
2. the actual dollar change at a clearly defined post-report price, such as the next close.

The 14.5% and 7.1% figures here are AP's July 23 closing moves. They should not be mislabeled as the exact overnight gap or the worst intraday decline.

## Portfolio heat should be measured before the catalyst

After the event, $540 is an observed calculation for the stated notional positions. Before the event, the trader could only choose a stress assumption and decide whether the resulting combined loss was acceptable.

That is the role of portfolio heat: turn separate positions into one account-level number before a shared catalyst. It does not predict the catalyst.

I used PositionMath's [portfolio heat calculator](https://positionmath.com/portfolio-heat-calculator/) only after showing the arithmetic. A useful tool should make the exposure easier to inspect, not hide the assumptions.

The retrospective lesson is simple. Two positions can have different percentage moves and still belong to one risk budget. Calculate each dollar result, add them, and compare the total with the account—not with the headline.
