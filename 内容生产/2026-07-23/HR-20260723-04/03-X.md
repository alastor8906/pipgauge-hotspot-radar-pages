# X thread draft

1/ CME's 1-Ounce Gold future is not the same thing as 1 standard lot of retail XAUUSD.

The label matters less than the ounces behind it.

2/ CME 1-Ounce Gold:

- Contract unit: 1 troy ounce
- Minimum tick: $0.25/oz
- Tick value: $0.25 per contract
- $25 gold move: about $25 per contract

3/ A common retail XAUUSD convention is 1.00 lot = 100 oz. Verify the actual broker spec.

Under that convention, a $25 move is roughly:

- 0.01 lot: $25
- 0.10 lot: $250
- 1.00 lot: $2,500

4/ Similar ounce exposure does not make the instruments interchangeable. Venue, tick rules, price feed, spread, commission, margin and settlement still differ.

5/ Longer trading hours also do not mean constant liquidity. CME publishes a maintenance window, and weekend depth can differ from the main session.

6/ Keep these separate:

- contract size
- tick value
- margin
- adverse-move loss

Leverage changes margin, not the P/L created by a fixed number of ounces.

7/ Before comparing any two gold products, translate both positions into ounces. That one step prevents most of the “one contract vs one lot” confusion.

Educational example only. Not financial advice.
