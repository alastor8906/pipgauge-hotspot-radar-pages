FTMO’s November 3 Notice: Add Your Planned Stop Risk Before You React

FTMO’s November 2 CFD trading update points to November 3 and asks traders to review open positions, position size, and risk per trade. That creates a useful calculation task. It does not create a market call.

This is not a post about candidates, election results, policy outcomes, or where any currency, index, commodity, or share may move. The practical question is narrower: if the stops already in a plan were reached, what is the planned risk across the positions already open?

FTMO says fast conditions can mean that orders, including stops, may fill differently from expected. That is a reason not to mistake a planned-risk calculation for a guaranteed loss limit. It is still useful to know the number the plan starts from.

Start with the sum, not a forecast

Suppose three positions already have independently chosen stop-risk amounts:

Existing planPlanned stop risk
Position AUSD 120
Position BUSD 80
Position CUSD 100
TotalUSD 300

The arithmetic is simply:

USD 120 + USD 80 + USD 100 = USD 300

If the same reader has chosen a separate USD 1,000 daily-risk ceiling for their own plan, the comparison is:

USD 300 ÷ USD 1,000 = 30%

That 30% is not an FTMO rule, a recommendation, an available-risk number, or a claim about an actual event loss. It is only the fraction of this teaching example’s self-chosen ceiling represented by three pre-defined planned stops.

The calculation is valuable because it keeps the inputs visible. It does not answer whether to open, close, hold, reduce, or increase a position. It does not say that any stop will be hit. And it cannot tell us how a market will behave.

Verify each input before adding it

Before treating a number as planned risk, separate four things that are often mixed together:

  1. The reader’s own account currency and contract specification.
  2. The position quantity already planned or held.
  3. The distance from entry to the reader’s independently chosen stop.
  4. Costs and execution outcomes that are not included in the teaching arithmetic.

For a forex position, a pip value and position-size check can establish the intended loss at the planned stop using the reader’s pair, account currency, contract convention and quantity. The same idea applies to points or other contract units, but the specification must come from the applicable platform or broker.

Only after those per-position inputs are verified does adding the planned losses make sense. A planned USD 120 is an input, not a promise that the actual loss cannot exceed USD 120. Spread, commission, financing, gaps, slippage, correlated moves, conversion and contract rules can all change a realized result.

The current notice is the hook—not a prediction

FTMO’s November 2 trading update identifies November 3 as an event-sensitive period and specifically asks readers to check open positions, size and risk. USAGov’s midterm overview independently confirms the November 2026 event context.

Neither source tells a reader what to trade. Neither establishes an expected range, a spread, a fill, a stop-loss outcome, or a firm-specific account conclusion. The useful response is not to manufacture certainty from a headline. It is to make the numbers already in a trading plan legible.

After the formula and limits are clear, PipGauge’s Portfolio Heat Calculator can be used to enter reader-verified planned risks together. PipGauge’s Pip Value and Position Size tools can help check the individual inputs first. They do not validate FTMO terms, interpret an event, estimate market conditions, pick a trade, or guarantee an execution.

A short expiry is part of the method

This event-specific framing is only for the current FTMO notice and ends by 12:00 ET on November 4. Retire it earlier if FTMO corrects or supersedes the update, if the notice does not apply to the account/product being discussed, or if the discussion has become generic volatility commentary.

The better evergreen habit is simpler: make each planned-loss input explicit, add them before reacting, and keep the plan distinct from the fill that may eventually occur.

Title: FTMO’s Nov. 3 update says to review open positions and risk per trade — how do you total the risk already on?

FTMO’s current Nov. 2 update flags Nov. 3 as an event-sensitive period and tells traders to check open positions, position size, and risk per trade. I’m not trying to turn that into a call on an election, FX, indices, or anything else.

The useful process question for me is: before reacting to a headline, how do you total the planned stop risk that is already on across positions?

For a simple teaching example, three independent plans might have maximum intended stop losses of $120, $80, and $100. The planned total is $300. If that trader has separately chosen a $1,000 daily-risk ceiling, that is 30% of the ceiling.

That does not mean $300 is guaranteed, that $1,000 is an FTMO rule, or that a stop will fill there. It leaves out spread, commission, slippage, gaps, conversion, correlation, and the specific contract rules. It is just a way to make the plan’s starting arithmetic visible.

My checklist would be:

  1. Verify each position’s account currency, contract value, quantity, and stop distance.
  2. Convert each planned stop to the same account-currency risk number.
  3. Add the planned numbers.
  4. Keep that total separate from any claim about actual fills, event conditions, or a firm’s account rules.

For people who use several positions around a scheduled headline: do you track planned stop risk as one portfolio total, and what inputs do you refuse to treat as fixed when conditions are fast?
