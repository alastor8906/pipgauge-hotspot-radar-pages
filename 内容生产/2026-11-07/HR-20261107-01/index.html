Canada Lost 4,700 Jobs in October. A USD/CAD Fixed-Risk Check Still Is Not a Forecast.

Statistics Canada reported on November 6 that employment was little changed in October at -4,700 and the unemployment rate rose to 6.7%. Reuters independently reported the same result. Those are completed data points. They do not say what CAD, USD/CAD, the Bank of Canada, or volatility will do next.

After a widely discussed jobs report, a useful question can remain entirely mechanical: if someone was already planning a USD/CAD trade, does the intended size still match the maximum amount of planned price risk they chose independently of the headline?

Start with the fixed-risk formula

planned dollar risk ÷ (planned stop in pips × pip value per standard lot)

For a transparent teaching setup, use a USD 10,000 account and USD 75 maximum planned price risk. For USD/CAD in a USD account, use approximately USD 10 per pip per standard lot only as a teaching assumption. Actual pip value depends on account currency, contract convention and product specification.

Planned stopArithmetic before costsApproximate standard-lot size
25 pipsUSD 75 ÷ (25 × USD 10)0.30 lot when rounded down
50 pipsUSD 75 ÷ (50 × USD 10)0.15 lot when rounded down
75 pipsUSD 75 ÷ (75 × USD 10)0.10 lot when rounded down

The table does not say USD/CAD will move 25, 50 or 75 pips after the jobs report. It shows only that the same planned dollar budget produces a smaller position when the planned stop is wider.

Keep the report separate from the account math

An auditable sequence is simple:

  1. Record the published facts: -4,700 employment change and 6.7% unemployment.
  2. Choose a maximum planned price-risk amount separately from the news.
  3. Choose a planned stop without calling it an expected move.
  4. Check the actual pip value and contract specification before calculating a size.

Commission, spread, slippage, gaps, minimum lot increments and platform rules can change an actual outcome. A planned stop is not a guaranteed exit.

After the facts and arithmetic are clear, use PipGauge’s pip-value calculator to verify the actual USD/CAD account currency and contract size, then use Position Size with the reader’s own inputs. The tools check inputs; they do not interpret Canada’s jobs report or forecast a market move.

This is a short-lived post-report refresh. It expires at 12:00 ET on November 8, on a Statistics Canada correction, or if later independently verified information creates a different calculation question.
