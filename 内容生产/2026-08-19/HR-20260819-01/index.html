Australia Jobs Data Is Due August 20. Recheck AUD/USD Risk Before the Release

Australia's July Labour Force release is scheduled for August 20 at 11:30 a.m. AEST. A scheduled release can change the market conversation, but it does not supply a correct AUD/USD position size, a guaranteed range, or a directional answer.

The useful question beforehand is simpler: if the amount an account is willing to lose stays fixed, what has to change when the planned stop changes?

Calendar context is not a forecast

The Australian Bureau of Statistics lists the July Labour Force release for August 20. Westpac's public calendar lists the same event and shows a 15,000 employment-change consensus against a 76,300 prior change, plus a 4.5% unemployment-rate consensus against a 4.4% prior rate.

Those are calendar inputs, not July results. They do not say whether AUD/USD will rise or fall, how far it might move, or whether a stop will fill at its intended price.

One loss budget, three stop distances

Assume a $10,000 USD account with a maximum planned loss of 1%, or $100. For AUD/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately $10 per pip.

lot size = dollar risk / (stop distance in pips × pip value per standard lot)

  • 25-pip stop: 100 / (25 × 10) = 0.40 lot.
  • 50-pip stop: 100 / (50 × 10) = 0.20 lot.
  • 75-pip stop: 100 / (75 × 10) = 0.1333 lot, or about 0.13 lot before broker rounding.

Each example targets the same $100 planned price loss before costs. Keeping 0.40 lot while moving a stop from 25 to 75 pips raises the planned price loss from $100 to $300. That is arithmetic, not an Australia-jobs forecast.

Keep the release separate from controllable inputs

  1. Fix the maximum dollar loss before selecting the lot size.
  2. Choose the stop from the trade structure, not from a desired lot size.
  3. Recalculate if the stop changes.
  4. Test spread, commission and slippage separately as a hypothetical stress case.
  5. When ABS publishes the result, remove all pre-release wording and verify official figures before making a result-based update.

For example, 0.20 lot has an approximate pip value of $2. At a 50-pip stop it targets $100 of price risk. A hypothetical extra two pips of adverse cost adds about $4; this is a stress calculation, not a claim about live execution. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill.

The PipGauge position size calculator can verify pair, account-currency and sizing inputs after the arithmetic. It cannot interpret the data, predict AUD/USD, or guarantee execution.

This is a refresh of the existing RBA/AUD/USD risk article, not a second same-structure macro post. Replace its August 11 RBA title, facts and low-volatility reference with the dated Australia-employment context. The pre-release version expires when the ABS release begins.
