# U.S. Payrolls Added 165,000. A Fixed-Risk Check Still Is Not a Forecast.

The U.S. employment report published on November 6 said total nonfarm payroll employment increased by 165,000 in October and the unemployment rate was 4.1%. Those are completed data points. They do not say what the dollar, EUR/USD, the Federal Reserve, or volatility will do next.

After a widely discussed payroll release, a useful question can remain entirely mechanical: if someone was already planning a trade, does the intended size still match the maximum amount of planned price risk they chose independently of the headline?

## Start with the fixed-risk formula

> planned dollar risk ÷ (planned stop in pips × pip value per standard lot)

For a transparent teaching setup, use a USD 10,000 account and USD 50 maximum planned price risk. For EUR/USD in a USD account, use approximately USD 10 per pip per standard lot only as a teaching assumption. The actual pip value depends on account currency, contract convention and product specification.

| Planned stop | Arithmetic before costs | Approximate standard-lot size |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30 pips | USD 50 ÷ (30 × USD 10) | 0.16 lot when rounded down |
| 60 pips | USD 50 ÷ (60 × USD 10) | 0.08 lot when rounded down |
| 90 pips | USD 50 ÷ (90 × USD 10) | 0.05 lot when rounded down |

The table does not say EUR/USD will move 30, 60 or 90 pips after payrolls. It shows only that the same planned dollar budget produces a smaller position when the planned stop is wider.

## Keep the report separate from the account math

An auditable sequence is simple:

1. Record the published fact: +165,000 payrolls and 4.1% unemployment.
2. Choose a maximum planned price-risk amount separately from the news.
3. Choose a planned stop without calling it an expected move.
4. Check the actual pip value and contract specification before calculating a size.

Commission, spread, slippage, gaps, minimum lot increments and platform rules can change an actual outcome. A planned stop is not a guaranteed exit.

The tools check inputs; they do not interpret the jobs report or forecast a market move.
