# X thread draft

1/ CME's planned single-stock futures use two contract multipliers:

- Standard: 100 shares
- Micro: 10 shares

The Micro is one tenth the exposure of the standard contract for the same stock. But neither is a fixed dollar size.

2/ Formula:

notional exposure = stock price × contract multiplier

At $50/share:
- Standard: $5,000
- Micro: $500

At $200:
- Standard: $20,000
- Micro: $2,000

3/ At $500/share:

- Standard: $50,000
- Micro: $5,000

“One contract” is incomplete without the multiplier and current share price.

4/ For a $200 stock, a 1% move is $2/share:

- Standard: about $200
- Micro: about $20

A 5% move is roughly $1,000 vs $100.

5/ Notional exposure is not margin. A lower margin deposit does not reduce the P/L generated by all 100 or 10 represented shares.

6/ Micro improves sizing granularity. It does not make risk automatically small. A $5k Micro exposure on a $10k account can still lose about $500 on a 10% adverse move.

7/ Verify final CME specs, launch status, margin and broker availability before using the contract. This is exposure math, not a directional trade or tax conclusion.

Educational example only. Not financial advice.
