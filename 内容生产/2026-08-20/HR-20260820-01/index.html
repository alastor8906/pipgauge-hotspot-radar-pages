FOMC Minutes Are Out. The Risk Question for EUR/USD Is Still a Number, Not a Forecast

The Federal Reserve has published the minutes of its July 28–29 meeting. The vote was 9–3 to keep the federal-funds target range at 3.50%–3.75%. The document records conditional inflation concerns, but it does not provide a EUR/USD price target, a correct stop distance, or a position size.

That is why the useful post-minutes question is not “what must EUR/USD do next?” It is whether a planned loss limit still holds when the stop distance changes.

The document is a fact, not a direction call

The Federal Reserve's minutes describe the meeting discussion. Associated Press and Reuters independently reported the released document and the 9–3 result. The wording matters for readers of the minutes; it does not turn a conditional policy discussion into a trade signal.

The same distinction applies to risk. A wider stop can be a deliberate choice, but it is not free. If the dollar amount an account is prepared to lose remains fixed, the position size has to fall as the stop gets wider.

One loss budget, three stop distances

Assume a USD 10,000 account with a maximum planned loss of 1%, or USD 100. For EUR/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

lot size = dollar risk / (stop distance in pips × pip value per standard lot)

  • 25-pip stop: 100 / (25 × 10) = 0.40 lot.
  • 50-pip stop: 100 / (50 × 10) = 0.20 lot.
  • 100-pip stop: 100 / (100 × 10) = 0.10 lot.

Each row targets the same USD 100 planned price loss before costs. Keeping 0.40 lot while moving a stop from 25 to 100 pips changes planned price risk from USD 100 to USD 400. That is arithmetic, not an interpretation of the minutes.

Keep facts, assumptions and costs separate

  1. Treat the 9–3 vote and the official minutes as dated facts.
  2. Set a maximum dollar loss before deciding on a lot size.
  3. Choose the stop from the trade structure, not from a preferred lot size.
  4. Recalculate size whenever the stop changes.
  5. Treat commission, spread and slippage as a separate, explicitly hypothetical stress case. A stop is not a guaranteed fill.

For example, 0.20 lot has an approximate pip value of USD 2. A 50-pip stop targets USD 100 of price risk before costs. A hypothetical extra two pips of adverse cost adds about USD 4. That is not a statement about live spreads or liquidity.

The PipGauge position size calculator can verify pair, account-currency and sizing inputs after the formula. It cannot interpret FOMC language, forecast EUR/USD, or guarantee execution.

This is a refresh of the existing EUR/USD FOMC risk article, not a second same-structure Fed post. Replace its earlier decision/pre-release title, opening, sources and countdown language with this released-minutes frame. The frame expires when newer U.S. data or the September FOMC decision supersedes it.

  • Reuters: inflation concerns in the minutes
