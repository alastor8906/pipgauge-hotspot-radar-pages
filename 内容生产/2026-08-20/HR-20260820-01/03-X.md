1/ The July 28–29 FOMC minutes are public. The vote was 9–3 to hold the target range at 3.50%–3.75%. That is a document fact, not a EUR/USD forecast.

2/ The risk question after a macro release is still numerical: if the loss budget is fixed, does the position size shrink when the planned stop is wider?

3/ Example: USD 10,000 account; 1% risk = USD 100. For EUR/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

4/ Same USD 100 budget: 25-pip stop = 0.40 lot. 50 pips = 0.20 lot. 100 pips = 0.10 lot.

5/ Keep 0.40 lot and widen a stop from 25 to 100 pips, and planned price risk rises from USD 100 to USD 400 before costs. Arithmetic, not a minutes interpretation.

6/ Spread, commission and slippage need their own hypothetical stress case. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill; a calculator checks inputs, not policy or price direction.

7/ Sources: Federal Reserve July 28–29 minutes; AP and Reuters recaps. Educational example only, not financial advice.
