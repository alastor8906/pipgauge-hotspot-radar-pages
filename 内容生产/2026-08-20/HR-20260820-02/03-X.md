1/ Australia’s July jobs result is public: employment fell 15,800 and the unemployment rate rose to 4.5%. The prior 15,000 figure was a forecast, not the result.

2/ Neither number tells AUD/USD where to go next. The usable risk question is: does size fall when a planned stop is wider and the loss budget stays fixed?

3/ Example: USD 10,000 account; 1% risk = USD 100. For AUD/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

4/ Same USD 100 budget: 25-pip stop = 0.40 lot. 50 pips = 0.20 lot. 75 pips = about 0.13 lot.

5/ Keep 0.40 lot and widen a stop from 25 to 75 pips, and planned price risk rises from USD 100 to USD 300 before costs. Arithmetic, not a jobs-report forecast.

6/ Spread, commission and slippage require a separate hypothetical stress case. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill, and a calculator checks inputs rather than predicting the report’s market effect.

7/ Sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics July Labour Force release; Reuters recap. Educational example only, not financial advice.
