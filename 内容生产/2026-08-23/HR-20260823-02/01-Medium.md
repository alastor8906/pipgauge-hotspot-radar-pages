# NVIDIA Reports August 26. Calculate the Overnight Gap You Can Absorb Before Guessing the Result

NVIDIA has scheduled its second-quarter fiscal 2027 results for August 26 after the market closes. The company says the conference call follows at 2 p.m. Pacific Time, or 5 p.m. Eastern Time. An independent current-week earnings calendar also lists NVIDIA for Wednesday.

Those dates are useful because they create a real deadline for a risk check. They do not tell anyone whether the report will be strong or weak, whether the shares will rise or fall, or whether holding through the release is appropriate. The part a calculator can help with is much narrower: how much overnight gap exposure does a stated share position create?

## Separate an earnings date from a price call

Earnings discussion often turns quickly into targets, predictions, or instructions to buy and sell. None of those is needed to measure exposure. The factual starting point is simply that a release is scheduled after the August 26 close.

Public discussion around the date includes the familiar question of whether to carry a position through results. That is a risk-process question, not evidence that one outcome is more likely. A useful answer should make the possible dollar exposure visible without relabeling a hypothetical gap as an expected move.

## A transparent teaching example

Assume a USD 10,000 account and eight shares at a **teaching anchor** of USD 218 per share. The anchor is not a live quote, a valuation opinion, or a forecast. It simply makes the arithmetic readable.

| Hypothetical overnight gap | Dollar move per share | Eight-share dollar change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5% | USD 10.90 | USD 87.20 |
| 10% | USD 21.80 | USD 174.40 |
| 15% | USD 32.70 | USD 261.60 |

The table works in either direction: it shows the absolute size of a hypothetical move, not a call about which direction will occur. A 10% downward gap on this eight-share example is about USD 174.40, which is 1.744% of the USD 10,000 account. A 15% move is about USD 261.60, or 2.616%. These percentages are exposure descriptions, not probability estimates.

## A stop-based share size is not an overnight guarantee

Now consider a separate, ordinary planned-loss calculation. If the maximum planned price loss is USD 100 and the trade structure uses a USD 12 stop distance per share:

`share size = dollar risk / stop distance per share = 100 / 12 = 8.33`

Rounding down gives eight shares. At the planned stop, that is about USD 96 of price risk before fees. This is a useful sizing rule for the structure chosen. It does not cap an overnight gap at USD 96.

If a report changes the price between the close and the next executable price, the stop can be bypassed. The gap table and the stop table therefore answer different questions. The first asks how much exposure is carried across a binary time window. The second asks how many shares fit a planned intraday loss at a chosen stop distance.

## Make concentration visible before the release

A share count can look modest in isolation while still dominating a smaller account. Before a scheduled company event, it is worth writing down four inputs: total account value, share count, cost or current reference price, and the largest dollar gap the account can tolerate. Then test a few plainly labeled scenarios rather than treating one number as a forecast.
