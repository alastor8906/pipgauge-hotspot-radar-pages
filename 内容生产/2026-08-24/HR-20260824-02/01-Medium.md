# NVIDIA Reports Wednesday. Calculate the Overnight Gap You Can Absorb Before Guessing the Result

NVIDIA has scheduled its second-quarter fiscal 2027 results for Wednesday, August 26, after the market closes. The company says written materials will appear at approximately 1:20 p.m. Pacific Time, followed by a 2 p.m. Pacific / 5 p.m. Eastern conference call. AP has described the report as one of the week's events that could matter for AI-related stocks.

Those facts create a deadline for a risk review. They do not tell anyone whether the results will be strong or weak, whether the shares will rise or fall, or whether carrying a position through the release is appropriate. The question that can be calculated is narrower: how much overnight-gap exposure is already embedded in a stated share position?

## Separate a reporting time from a price call

Earnings discussions often turn into targets, options narratives, and instructions to buy or sell. None of that is needed to make exposure visible. The factual starting point is only that NVIDIA is scheduled to report after the August 26 close.

Public discussion around the date includes the familiar question of whether to hold through results. That is a risk-process question, not evidence for one outcome. A useful answer should show possible dollar exposure without relabelling a hypothetical gap as an expected move.

## A transparent teaching example

Assume a USD 10,000 account and eight shares at a **teaching anchor** of USD 218 per share. The anchor is not a live quote, valuation view or forecast. It makes the arithmetic readable.

| Hypothetical overnight gap | Dollar move per share | Eight-share dollar change |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5% | USD 10.90 | USD 87.20 |
| 10% | USD 21.80 | USD 174.40 |
| 15% | USD 32.70 | USD 261.60 |

The table works in either direction: it shows the absolute size of a hypothetical move, not a call about which direction will occur. A 10% downward gap in this eight-share example is about USD 174.40, or 1.744% of the USD 10,000 account. A 15% move is about USD 261.60, or 2.616%. Those percentages describe exposure; they are not probability estimates.

## A stop-based share size is not an overnight guarantee

Now take a separate, ordinary planned-loss calculation. If maximum planned price loss is USD 100 and the trade structure uses a USD 12 stop distance per share:

`share size = dollar risk / stop distance per share = 100 / 12 = 8.33`

Rounding down gives eight shares. At the planned stop, that is about USD 96 of price risk before fees. This can be a useful sizing rule for the structure chosen. It does not cap an overnight gap at USD 96.

If a report changes the price between the close and the next executable price, the stop can be bypassed. The gap table and the stop table answer different questions. The first asks how much exposure is carried across a binary time window. The second asks how many shares fit a planned intraday loss at a chosen stop distance.

## Make concentration visible before the materials are public

A share count can look modest in isolation while still dominating a smaller account. Before a scheduled company event, write down four inputs: total account value, share count, cost or reference price, and the largest dollar gap the account could tolerate. Then test a few plainly labelled scenarios instead of treating one percentage as a forecast.
