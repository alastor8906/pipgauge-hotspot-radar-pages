The RBA Decision Is Scheduled. AUD/USD Risk Still Starts With a Number, Not a Forecast

The Reserve Bank of Australia has scheduled its Monetary Policy Decision Statement for Tuesday, September 29 at 2:30 pm AEST, followed by a 3:30 pm media conference. Reuters and ABC News have both covered the decision week. Those are useful timing facts. They do not provide an AUD/USD target, a correct stop distance, or a position size.

That distinction matters whenever a calendar event pulls attention toward a currency pair. The useful pre-decision question is not what the RBA must do or where AUD/USD must trade afterward. It is whether a planned loss limit still holds when the stop distance changes.

One dollar budget, three planned stops

Assume a USD 10,000 account with a maximum planned price loss of 1%, or USD 100. For AUD/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

lot size = dollar risk / (planned stop in pips × pip value per standard lot)

  • 25-pip planned stop: 100 / (25 × 10) = 0.40 lot.
  • 50-pip planned stop: 100 / (50 × 10) = 0.20 lot.
  • 75-pip planned stop: 100 / (75 × 10) = 0.1333 lot, or about 0.13 lot before broker rounding.

Each row aims at the same USD 100 planned price loss before costs. Keeping 0.40 lot while changing the planned stop from 25 to 75 pips would change planned price risk from USD 100 to USD 300 before costs. That is arithmetic, not an interpretation of the coming statement.

The calendar is a clock, not a signal

The RBA's calendar gives a specific statement time. Reuters and ABC News provide current decision-week context. ForexFactory and public Reddit discussion make the event easier to discover. None of those sources decide whether AUD/USD should be bought or sold, how far it will move, or how a stop will fill.

Keep the controllable inputs separate from the news:

  1. Set the maximum dollar loss before choosing the size.
  2. Select a planned stop from the trade structure, not from a preferred lot size.
  3. Recalculate size whenever the planned stop changes.
  4. Treat spread, commission and slippage as a separate, explicitly hypothetical stress case.
  5. Recheck the event language as soon as the RBA statement is published.

For example, 0.20 lot has an approximate pip value of USD 2. A hypothetical extra two pips of adverse execution cost would add about USD 4. That is a stress calculation, not a claim about live RBA-session spreads, liquidity or fills. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill.

After the formula and assumptions are clear, the PipGauge position size calculator can verify pair, account-currency and sizing inputs. It does not interpret RBA policy, forecast AUD/USD, or guarantee execution.

Replace its August hold, immediate-reaction, source and USD 50 example sections with this September 29 decision-clock frame. The frame expires when the statement is published or the RBA changes its schedule.
