Goat Funded Trader’s 3% news-profit cap: the purchase date comes before the arithmetic

Draft status: awaiting_human_review. This is educational rule-reading, not a recommendation to buy an account, trade a news release, or expect a particular outcome.

Goat Funded Trader updated its public news-trading help article on 10 November 2026. The useful question is not whether a headline will move a market. It is much narrower: if a trade opens or closes in the firm’s stated high-impact-news window, which percentage cap applies to the profit from that one trade?

The answer begins with the account’s purchase date.

According to the current Goat Funded Trader help article, a trade opened or closed from five minutes before through five minutes after a high-impact, red-folder release can be subject to a per-trade profit cap. Accounts purchased on or after 10 November 2026 use a cap of 3% of the initial balance per trade. Accounts purchased before that date remain on a 1% cap. The page says this applies in both the challenge and funded phases, and that a funded account follows the purchase date of the challenge from which it came.

That makes this a cohort rule, not a market view. A reset after 10 November does not, by itself, move an older purchase into the new cohort. The page also says profit above the applicable ceiling is removed during review rather than treated as a breach. The current official wording is decisive, so the plan, purchase date and account agreement need to be checked before relying on any example below.

First calculate the rule ceiling, not a trade idea

The rule arithmetic is small but easy to misread:

initial account balance × applicable news-profit percentage = maximum profit retained from one qualifying trade

Using the firm’s published USD 100,000 example:

Purchase-date cohortPer-trade capArithmeticMaximum retained profit
Purchased on or after 10 November 20263%USD 100,000 × 0.03USD 3,000
Purchased before 10 November 20261%USD 100,000 × 0.01USD 1,000

The firm illustrates a USD 4,500 profit on one trade opened or closed in the specified window. Under the newer cohort, USD 3,000 is retained and USD 1,500 is removed. Under the earlier cohort, USD 1,000 is retained and USD 3,500 is removed.

Those figures are rule examples. They are not an expected move, a suitable target, an instruction to hold a position through data, or a statement about what any instrument will do. They also are not a daily-loss limit, a payout amount, a total-account profit limit or a per-trade risk budget.

Keep three different numbers separate

The cleanest way to avoid mixing rules is to label the numbers before doing more math.

  1. Initial balance is the base used by the quoted 1% or 3% news-profit rule.
  2. Per-trade retained-profit cap is a ceiling applied to an eligible trade in the stated time window; it is not a loss limit.
  3. Planned price risk is the amount a trader independently chooses to risk between entry and a planned stop. It is not set by the news-profit cap and does not guarantee a fill.

For example, a reader could have a USD 100,000 account in the newer cohort, making the quoted ceiling USD 3,000. Separately, the reader might model USD 500 of planned price risk. At a purely hypothetical 1R, 2R or 3R plan, the corresponding planned gross profit is USD 500, USD 1,000 or USD 1,500. That comparison does not forecast price, endorse a risk/reward ratio or establish eligibility. It simply prevents an account rule from being mistaken for a position-sizing rule.

Before any trade, costs, gaps, slippage, partial fills, lot minimums and the exact product terms can make realized results differ from a plan. The official rule is also about the timing of opening or closing a trade around a high-impact event. A generic calculator cannot determine whether a particular release is covered, whether an account belongs to the new cohort, or how the firm will apply its review.

Where PipGauge fits — after the official rule check

The first stop is the official rule and the reader’s own purchase date. Once that is clear, PipGauge can help with a separate task: checking reader-entered account arithmetic rather than interpreting the rule.

  • PipGauge Risk/Reward can distinguish a planned price-risk amount from a hypothetical planned reward.
  • PipGauge Position Size can calculate a reader’s planned quantity from their own entry, stop, account currency and risk budget.
  • PipGauge Challenge Simulator can organise reader-entered account targets and loss constraints alongside, but not in place of, the firm’s current rulebook.
  • PositionMath Prop Firm Simulator is a secondary cross-check for reader-entered constraints.

Put any calculator link after the purchase-date rule, the 1% versus 3% comparison and the warning that the cap applies to one qualifying trade’s profit. A calculator does not validate an account, a news event, a firm’s policy, a trade outcome or a payout.

A quick verification checklist

Before using the 3% headline, verify all of the following in the current official materials and, where relevant, the account dashboard:

  • Is the account’s original purchase date on or after 10 November 2026?
  • Is the account a challenge-derived funded account that inherits the original challenge date?
  • Does the trade open or close inside the firm’s stated five-minute-before / five-minute-after window?
  • Is the event classified as high impact under the firm’s referenced calendar definition?
  • Are you calculating a per-trade profit ceiling from the initial balance, rather than a loss budget or payout cap?

Independent rule tracker BestProps records the 10 November change and its purchase-date distinction. It is a useful second check on why the change is current, but the firm’s official page remains the controlling source.

This dated angle expires quickly. Stop using it if the help article changes, the effective date or scope changes, the page can no longer be verified, or the short post-update window ends. The goal is accurate rule reading—not turning a news-event restriction into a trade thesis.
