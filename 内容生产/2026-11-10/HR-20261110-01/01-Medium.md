# U.S. CPI Rose 0.4% in October. A Fixed-Risk Check Still Is Not a Forecast.

The U.S. Consumer Price Index report published on November 10 said the all-items CPI rose 0.4% in October on a seasonally adjusted basis, after a 0.6% increase in September. The core measure, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3%. The Bureau of Labor Statistics also says it cannot publish many October 2026 twelve-month changes because October 2025 survey data were unavailable.

Those are completed data points. They do not tell anyone what EUR/USD, the dollar, gold, an index, interest rates, or volatility will do next. A CPI headline is not a position-size instruction.

There is still a useful mechanical question after a heavily watched release: if a person had already chosen a maximum amount of planned price risk, how does a wider planned stop change the quantity they would enter into a calculator? That is a budgeting question. It is deliberately separate from trying to anticipate the next candle.

## Start with the fixed-risk formula

> planned dollar risk ÷ (planned stop in pips × pip value per standard lot)

Use a transparent teaching setup: a USD 10,000 account and USD 50 maximum planned price risk. For EUR/USD in a USD account, use approximately USD 10 per pip per standard lot only as a teaching assumption. Actual pip value depends on account currency, quote convention, contract size, and the product specification.

| Planned stop | Arithmetic before costs | Approximate standard-lot size |
|---|---:|---:|
| 25 pips | USD 50 ÷ (25 × USD 10) | 0.20 lot |
| 50 pips | USD 50 ÷ (50 × USD 10) | 0.10 lot |
| 75 pips | USD 50 ÷ (75 × USD 10) | about 0.06 lot when rounded down |

The table does not claim EUR/USD moved, will move, or should move 25, 50, or 75 pips after CPI. Those are reader-chosen teaching inputs. It only shows that the same planned dollar budget implies a smaller planned quantity when the stop input is wider.

## Keep the news fact separate from account math

An auditable sequence is short:

1. Record the published facts: October CPI +0.4% month over month and core CPI +0.3%.
2. Pick a maximum planned price-risk amount independently of the headline.
3. Pick a planned stop without calling it an expected CPI range.
4. Verify the actual pair, account currency, pip value, contract specification, and any account-level daily-loss rule before entering a quantity.

The fourth step matters because a planned loss is not necessarily the loss an account would realize. Spread, commission, slippage, gaps, minimum size increments, platform constraints, and firm rules can change the result. A planned stop is not a guaranteed fill. A daily-loss limit is also a separate account rule, not a substitute for calculating risk per position.

After the facts and arithmetic are clear, use [PipGauge’s pip-value calculator](https://pipgauge.com/pip-value-calculator/) to check the actual pair, account currency, and contract size. Then use [PipGauge Position Size](https://pipgauge.com/position-size-calculator/) with the reader’s own risk and stop inputs. If the account has a daily-loss ceiling, treat that as a separate budget check rather than evidence that a single trade is safe. The calculators check inputs; they do not interpret CPI, validate a firm rule, forecast a market move, or promise an execution outcome.
