1/ Australia’s August annual CPI was published at 4.0%, up from 3.5% in July. That is a released fact, not an AUD/USD forecast.

2/ After an inflation headline, a useful question can be numerical: if a planned loss budget is fixed, should position size shrink when the planned stop is wider?

3/ Teaching inputs: USD 10,000 account; 1% planned price risk = USD 100. AUD/USD in a USD account is approximately USD 10/pip per standard lot.

4/ Same USD 100 budget: 40-pip planned stop = 0.25 lot. 80 pips = about 0.12 lot. 120 pips = about 0.08 lot before rounding.

5/ Keep 0.25 lot and change a planned stop from 40 to 120 pips, and planned price risk rises from about USD 100 to about USD 300 before costs. Arithmetic, not an inflation view.

6/ The 40/80/120-pip figures are teaching inputs, not an expected CPI-day range. Spread, commission and slippage need a separately labelled hypothetical stress check; a planned stop is not a guaranteed fill.

7/ Sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics August CPI release and ABC News Australia same-day reporting. Educational example only; not financial advice.
