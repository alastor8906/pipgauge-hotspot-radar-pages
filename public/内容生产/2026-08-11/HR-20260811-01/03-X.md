# X thread draft — awaiting human review

1/ RBA held the cash rate at 4.35% in a unanimous decision. The useful AUD/USD question is not “up or down?” It is: does your dollar-risk limit stay fixed when your stop distance changes?

2/ Example: $10,000 USD account, 0.5% risk = $50. For AUD/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is about $10 per pip.

3/ Same $50 budget: 20-pip stop = 0.25 lot. 35-pip stop ≈ 0.14 lot. 50-pip stop = 0.10 lot. Wider stop, smaller size.

4/ If you keep 0.25 lot and widen the stop from 20 to 50 pips, planned loss rises from $50 to $125 before costs. The market thesis did not do that; the sizing choice did.

5/ Independent coverage reported an immediate AUD/USD move of less than 0.1%. That is a timestamped observation, not proof that later volatility will be low.

6/ Add spread, commission and slippage as a separate stress case. A stop is not a guaranteed fill. A calculator verifies inputs; it does not forecast AUD/USD.

7/ Sources: RBA decision and August Statement on Monetary Policy; FOREX.com for the timestamped immediate reaction. Educational example only, not financial advice.
