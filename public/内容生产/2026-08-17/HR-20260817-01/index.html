FOMC Minutes Arrive August 19. Recheck EUR/USD Risk Before the Release

The Federal Reserve is scheduled to publish the minutes of its July 28–29 meeting at 2:00 p.m. Eastern Time on August 19. The minutes can add detail to a meeting that held rates steady and included three dissents, but they do not supply a price target, a stop distance, or a position size for EUR/USD.

That distinction is useful before a scheduled release. The question is not what the minutes must do to the dollar. It is whether the same account-loss limit still holds if the stop distance used for a trade changes.

One loss budget, three stop distances

Assume a $10,000 USD account with a maximum planned loss of 1%, or $100. For EUR/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately $10 per pip.

lot size = dollar risk / (stop distance in pips × pip value per standard lot)

  • A 25-pip stop: 100 / (25 × 10) = 0.40 lot.
  • A 50-pip stop: 100 / (50 × 10) = 0.20 lot.
  • A 100-pip stop: 100 / (100 × 10) = 0.10 lot.

Each example aims at the same $100 planned loss before transaction costs. Keeping 0.40 lot while moving a stop from 25 to 100 pips would raise the planned loss from $100 to $400. That is a sizing change, not an inference from the Fed.

Minutes are a document release, not a trade signal

The Fed calendar establishes the date and time. AP’s current week-ahead coverage establishes why the meeting is being watched: the July decision left rates unchanged, while three officials dissented. The minutes may provide more context, but their content is not known before publication.

For that reason, a responsible pre-release checklist uses controllable inputs:

  1. Set the maximum dollar loss first.
  2. Choose a stop from the trade structure, rather than fitting one to a desired lot size.
  3. Recalculate size when the stop changes.
  4. Keep commission, spread and slippage as a separate stress case.
  5. Recheck any event wording as soon as the official minutes are available.

For example, a 0.20-lot EUR/USD position has an approximate pip value of $2. At a 50-pip stop it plans for $100 of price risk. A hypothetical additional two pips of adverse execution cost would add about $4. That is not a prediction of a 2-pip spread or a claim about live liquidity; it is simply a way to avoid hiding costs inside the stop calculation.

The PipGauge position size calculator can be used after the arithmetic to check pair, account-currency and sizing inputs. It cannot interpret the minutes, forecast EUR/USD, or guarantee a stop fill.

The August 19 release may change the conversation. It does not need to change the amount an account has decided it can lose.
