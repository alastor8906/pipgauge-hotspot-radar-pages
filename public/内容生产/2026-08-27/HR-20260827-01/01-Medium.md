# NVIDIA’s Results Are Public. Now Calculate the Position Exposure, Not the Next Price

NVIDIA has released its second-quarter fiscal 2027 results. The company reported USD 96.2 billion in revenue, GAAP diluted earnings per share of USD 2.46, and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of USD 2.22. AP independently reported the released results. Axios reported that the stock was up more than 7% on Thursday morning.

Those are dated facts. They do not establish where the stock goes next, whether a position should be opened or closed, or what a future move is likely to be.

The more useful result-day question is narrower: if a share position is already in an account, what dollar amount does a clearly labelled percentage move represent?

## Use the reported reaction as arithmetic, not a forecast

Take a USD 10,000 account holding eight shares at a **USD 218 teaching anchor**. The anchor is not a live quote, valuation opinion, target, or recommendation. It only makes the calculation readable.

| Absolute move | Dollar move per share | Eight-share dollar change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reported approximately 7% reaction | USD 15.26 | USD 122.08 |
| Symmetric 5% stress check | USD 10.90 | USD 87.20 |
| Symmetric 10% stress check | USD 21.80 | USD 174.40 |

The 7% row uses the reported approximate result-day reaction as an exposure example. The other rows are symmetric stress checks. None estimates the next session, future volatility, or the probability of a gain or loss.

## A planned stop calculation answers a different question

Suppose a separate intraday plan allows USD 100 of price risk and uses a USD 12 stop distance per share:

share size = dollar risk / stop distance = 100 / 12 = 8.33

Rounding down gives eight shares. At the planned stop, that is roughly USD 96 of price risk before fees. This can be a useful sizing rule for a stated intraday structure. It is not insurance against a result-related gap or a guarantee of an exit price.

Keeping the two calculations separate matters. The percentage table makes a current holding visible after a reported move. The stop calculation sizes a planned loss under a chosen exit distance. They answer different questions and neither requires a view on NVIDIA.
