# Jackson Hole Is This Week. Your EUR/USD Risk Still Needs a Number, Not a Forecast

The 2026 Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium is scheduled for August 27 through August 29. The Federal Reserve calendar lists Chair Kevin Warsh for keynote remarks at 10:00 a.m. Eastern Time on Friday, August 28. Reuters has reported on the current attention around the speech in a bond-market context.

Those are useful facts to check. They are not an EUR/USD forecast, a policy forecast, or a reason to make a position larger. A scheduled event can be important while still leaving the direction of a currency move unknown.

That distinction matters in the final days before a policy event. The popular question is often what a speaker will say. No risk calculator can answer it. The more useful question is whether a fixed loss limit still holds if a trade structure calls for a wider planned stop.

## The event clock and the risk budget are different things

The Kansas City Fed supplies the symposium dates. The Federal Reserve supplies the keynote time. Reuters supplies independent, current context for why the event is receiving attention. None of those sources establishes where EUR/USD will trade after the remarks.

Treat the event clock as a prompt to review a process, not as a signal. If a trader normally risks a fixed dollar amount, a wider stop should mean a smaller position. If the position is not changed, the amount at risk changes even if the account's rule did not.

## One fixed loss budget, three possible stop distances

Assume a USD 10,000 account and a maximum planned price loss of 1%, or USD 100. For EUR/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

`lot size = dollar risk / (stop distance in pips × pip value per standard lot)`

| Planned stop | Calculation | Approximate size |
|---|---:|---:|
| 25 pips | `100 / (25 × 10)` | 0.40 lot |
| 50 pips | `100 / (50 × 10)` | 0.20 lot |
| 100 pips | `100 / (100 × 10)` | 0.10 lot |

Each row targets the same USD 100 of planned price risk before costs. The table does not say that 25, 50, or 100 pips is the right stop. Stop placement belongs to a trade structure, and that structure may be unsuitable around a scheduled event. The arithmetic only says what must change if the stop distance changes but the loss limit does not.

For example, keeping 0.40 lot while widening the plan from 25 to 100 pips changes planned price risk from roughly USD 100 to USD 400 before costs. That result does not depend on a view about Warsh or EUR/USD. It is simply the consequence of multiplying four times as many pips by the same pip value.

## Costs and fills require a separate caveat

Commission, spread and slippage are separate from the planned price-risk table. At 0.20 lot, the approximate pip value is USD 2. A hypothetical additional two pips of adverse cost would add about USD 4. That is a teaching stress case, not a claim about live Jackson Hole liquidity or how any broker will fill an order.

A stop is also not a guaranteed fill. If a market reprices quickly, the next executable price can be away from the planned level. The answer is not a prediction that every event must be traded, avoided, or traded in a particular direction. It is a reminder not to let a position silently become larger in dollar-risk terms merely because the intended stop widened.
