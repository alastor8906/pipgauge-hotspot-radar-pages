# The Fed Held at 3.50%–3.75%. The Useful Number Is Still Your Dollar Risk

The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.50%–3.75% on July 29, 2026. The vote was 9–3, with three members preferring a 25-basis-point increase.

That split is a strong news hook, but it is not a trading direction. A more durable question is: if EUR/USD moves quickly after a central-bank decision, how much position can a fixed loss budget support?

Assume a $10,000 USD account and a maximum planned loss of $100. For EUR/USD, one standard lot is approximately $10 per pip.

`lot size = dollar risk ÷ (stop distance × pip value)`

- 25-pip stop: `100 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.40 lot`
- 50-pip stop: 0.20 lot
- 100-pip stop: 0.10 lot

The wider stop does not authorize a larger loss. It requires a smaller position if the $100 budget is meant to remain fixed.

## Spread is a separate event cost

At a 1-pip spread, opening cost is roughly $4, $2 and $1 for those three sizes. At 3 pips, it becomes about $12, $6 and $3. Commission and slippage are separate again.

These are planning figures, not a promise that a stop fills at its requested price. A fast market can produce a worse fill. The useful stress test is whether $150 or $200 would still be acceptable if the planned $100 becomes a realized loss above plan.

The 9–3 vote can change the headline. It should not quietly change the account’s maximum loss.
