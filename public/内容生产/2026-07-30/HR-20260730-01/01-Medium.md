# BoE Held at 3.75%, but the 6–3 Vote Changes the Risk Conversation

The Bank of England kept Bank Rate at 3.75% on July 30. The more useful new number is the vote: six members supported the hold, while three preferred an immediate 25-basis-point increase to 4%.

In June, the vote to hold had been 7–2. The policy rate did not change, but the split widened by one member.

That distinction matters without implying a direction for GBP/USD. A central-bank headline and a trading position are different inputs: the vote describes policy disagreement; lot size determines what a market move does to the account.

## Translate pips into dollars first

For a USD-denominated account, a standard 100,000-unit GBP/USD lot is approximately $10 per pip. Broker specifications and account currencies can change that value, so it should be verified before use.

At that common specification, a one-lot position would change by approximately:

| GBP/USD move | Approximate P&L at 1.00 lot |
|---:|---:|
| 25 pips | $250 |
| 50 pips | $500 |
| 100 pips | $1,000 |

The sign depends on the position direction. These are stress scenarios, not a forecast of what sterling should do after the decision.

## Keep the dollar risk fixed

Assume a $10,000 account with a maximum planned loss of 1%, or $100.

The basic relationship is:

`lot size = dollar risk ÷ (stop distance × pip value per standard lot)`

Using $10 per pip gives:

| Stop distance | Dollar-risk budget | Approximate position size |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 25 pips | $100 | 0.40 lot |
| 50 pips | $100 | 0.20 lot |
| 100 pips | $100 | 0.10 lot |

If the distance needed for an event-risk trade doubles, the position has to halve to preserve the same planned loss. Keeping the original lot size while widening the stop quietly increases account risk.

## Event spreads belong in the calculation

The table is a clean baseline. Around policy releases, spreads may widen and stops may fill beyond the intended price.

At the three position sizes above, a one-pip spread costs roughly $4, $2 and $1. A five-pip spread costs about $20, $10 and $5. Those amounts do not predict volatility, but they show why the quoted spread should be checked separately from the stop distance.

The sequence is:

1. Verify the broker's GBP/USD contract and pip value.
2. Choose a dollar-risk ceiling.
3. Select a defensible stop or stress distance.
4. Reduce the lot size as that distance grows.
5. Add the observed spread and allow for slippage.

PipGauge's [pip value calculator](https://pipgauge.com/pip-value-calculator/) can verify the first input, while its [position size calculator](https://pipgauge.com/position-size-calculator/) can work backward from the dollar-risk limit. The arithmetic comes first because a calculator cannot decide whether the policy event is tradable.

The Bank's 6–3 vote is evidence of a more divided committee, not evidence that GBP/USD must rise or fall. The controllable number remains the amount at risk if the market moves against the position.
