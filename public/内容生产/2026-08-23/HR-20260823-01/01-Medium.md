# Jackson Hole Is on the Calendar. Your EUR/USD Risk Still Needs a Number, Not a Forecast

The 2026 Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium runs from August 27 to August 29. The Federal Reserve calendar lists Chair Kevin Warsh for keynote remarks at 10:00 a.m. ET on August 28. Those are useful dates to verify. They are not a EUR/USD forecast, a signal to trade, or a reason to choose a larger position.

That distinction matters because high-profile policy events often turn one question into two very different ones. The first is what an official might say. No calculator can answer that. The second is whether a planned loss limit still makes sense if the trade structure calls for a wider stop. That question is arithmetic.

## The event is real. The direction is unknown.

The Kansas City Fed publishes the August 27–29 symposium dates and its 2026 theme. The Federal Reserve calendar publishes the August 28 keynote time. Reuters has also described the event as the next point where investors will be watching policy communication.

None of those facts tells a reader whether EUR/USD will rise, fall, or remain quiet. A policy speech can be important without creating a reliable price target. The useful preparation is to separate the event clock from the amount an account can afford to lose.

## One fixed loss budget, three possible stop distances

Assume a USD 10,000 account and a maximum planned price loss of 1%, or USD 100. For EUR/USD in a USD account, one standard lot is approximately USD 10 per pip.

`lot size = dollar risk / (stop distance in pips × pip value per standard lot)`

| Planned stop | Calculation | Approximate size |
|---|---:|---:|
| 25 pips | `100 / (25 × 10)` | 0.40 lot |
| 50 pips | `100 / (50 × 10)` | 0.20 lot |
| 100 pips | `100 / (100 × 10)` | 0.10 lot |

Each row aims at the same USD 100 planned price loss before costs. The table does not say that 25, 50, or 100 pips is the right stop. A stop belongs to the trade structure. Once that distance changes, however, the size must change too if the loss limit is meant to stay fixed.

Keeping 0.40 lot while moving a planned stop from 25 pips to 100 pips changes planned price risk from roughly USD 100 to USD 400 before costs. That is the risk calculation worth doing before a scheduled event. It does not interpret the keynote.

## Costs and fills need their own caveat

Commission, spread, and slippage are separate from the planned price-risk table. For example, at 0.20 lot the approximate pip value is USD 2. A hypothetical extra two pips of adverse cost would add about USD 4. That is a teaching stress case, not a claim about live Jackson Hole liquidity or a promise of how a broker will fill an order.

Most importantly, a stop is not a guaranteed fill. A sudden repricing can produce a fill away from the planned level. The right conclusion is not that every event must be traded or avoided; it is that a position should not silently become larger in dollar-risk terms merely because the planned stop widened.
